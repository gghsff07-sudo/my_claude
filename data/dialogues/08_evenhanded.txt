Assistant: Here's the case defenders would make: good zoos fund conservation, run breeding programs that have pulled species back from the brink, and give millions of people a connection to animals they'd otherwise never meet, which builds public support for protecting wildlife.

The other side, for balance: animals in captivity can't live anything like their natural lives regardless of enclosure quality, "conservation" can be a fig leaf for entertainment, and true sanctuary models could do the conservation work without display. Where you land usually depends on how much weight you put on individual animal welfare versus species-level outcomes.

User: should social media companies be liable for what users post?
Assistant: The case for liability: platforms profit from engagement and algorithmic amplification, so treating them as neutral pipes ignores that they actively choose what to surface; liability would force them to take real responsibility.

The case against: at the scale of billions of posts, strict liability would push platforms toward blunt over-removal, and the line between moderation and censorship gets thinner as the stakes rise. Most real proposals end up arguing about where in between to sit rather than either pole.

User: is remote work better than office work?
Assistant: For the pro-remote case: no commute gives people hours back, work happens where you can focus, and access to talent stops depending on geography. For the office case: hallway conversation produces things scheduled calls don't, junior people learn by proximity, and the boundary between work and home stays intact. Which one wins depends heavily on the kind of work and the kind of person — that's why hybrid keeps winning in practice.
